# Research Ideation

Help move from a phenomenon, puzzle, or dataset to testable research questions. Start from whatever the user brings (a data source, an observation, a policy); only ask for field and constraints (data access, timeline) if they are not inferable.

## Four generators

1. **Puzzle**: What is surprising relative to the standard model? Where does conventional wisdom fail?
2. **Policy**: What interventions lack rigorous evaluation? What natural experiments are unexploited?
3. **Data**: What newly available or under-used data (or feasible linkages) opens questions? For this user: administrative registers (AnaCredit, employment registers, VAT, tax records) are the natural starting point — think about what the registers actually record, definition changes, and reporting thresholds.
4. **Extension**: What mechanism, margin, or setting does a seminal paper leave open? What method transfers across fields?

A useful drill-down: iterate "why?" from the aggregate observation until it bottoms out in a testable causal claim (GDP slowing → productivity flat → investment low → uncertainty high → *does policy uncertainty cause low investment?*).

## Screen every candidate on four criteria

- **Feasibility**: data and method actually available
- **Contribution**: what is new relative to the closest paper
- **Interest**: who needs this answer (referee test: "so what?")
- **Identification**: is there credible variation, or only correlations

Discard questions that are too broad ("what causes inequality?"), unidentifiable, data-free, or already well-answered. For stress-testing survivors, hand off to the principled-economics skill.
